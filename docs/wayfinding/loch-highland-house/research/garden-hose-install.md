# Garden hose install on cedar siding

## Verdict

Mount the ELEY 1041 wall plate to two framing studs, never to the cedar alone. Its four holes are 16 inches on center horizontally and 7-5/16 inches vertically. On cedar lap siding, use rigid pass-through spacers bearing on the sheathing so tightening the four stainless lag screws cannot flatten or split the laps; ELEY's current mounting guide prefers this over clamping any siding between the plate and framing. The four included 1/2-inch lap-siding standoffs can level the plate where the wall assembly permits them to bear firmly, but they do not solve unknown 24-inch stud spacing or fragile/unbacked siding.

A mounting block is unnecessary with 16-inch framing and sound sheathing. With 24-inch or 19.2-inch framing, use ELEY's platform method, or have a carpenter open the wall and add blocking. Seal each pass-through at the sheathing with premium exterior silicone as ELEY directs. Do not merely caulk around the plate: Western Red Cedar Lumber Association guidance says sealant is not a substitute for flashing and must not block drainage.

This is a substantial live load. ELEY's official product feed assigns the standard no-hose reel a 9,072 g (20 lb) product weight. Its 125-foot 5/8-inch hose weighs 34 lb filled, and the included 6.5-foot inlet hose gains about 1 lb when filled. The installed assembly is therefore about **55 lb with water**, before someone pulls on a hose extended perpendicular to the wall. Four fasteners into two studs are warranted; siding anchors are not.

## Mounting procedure

1. **Choose the location and reel direction.** Keep the plate within five feet of the faucet, per the ELEY manual, while allowing the included 6.5-foot leader hose to hang without a sharp bend. Parallel assembly pulls along the wall; perpendicular assembly pulls away from it. Set the plate roughly 12 inches above knee height and leave room to crank and remove the reel for winter.
2. **Find and prove two studs.** Use the inside-to-outside transfer method below. Confirm both centers with a small pilot hole before making large siding holes. Check the cavity for plumbing and wiring from inside. The wall plate requires 16-inch centers in its usual horizontal orientation.
3. **Mark the plate.** Level the 19-1/8 by 8-9/16-inch plate and mark all four holes. They are 16 inches horizontally by 7-5/16 inches vertically, each 7/16 inch in diameter. Both holes in each column must land on the same stud.
4. **Do not clamp the cedar laps.** ELEY's detailed guide says siding is nonstructural and recommends a pass-through installation. Drill the four small pilot holes through the cedar first, then verify continuous wood cutting for 1-3/4 inches into each stud. Stop and relocate if a hole misses. Enlarge only the cedar at each location for a rigid spacer, without cutting the sheathing. Measure each stand-off length from the face of the sheathing to the plane where the plate will sit flat. Cut four pieces of ELEY's specified schedule 80 conduit, or schedule 40 where schedule 80 is unavailable. The included 1/2-inch lap spacers are suitable only if they produce the same firm, flat bearing without loading a sloped or unsupported lap.
5. **Select lag length from the actual wall.** ELEY's formula is spacer length plus 1-3/4 inches for a direct pass-through installation. The current reel includes four 1/4 by 2-1/2-inch hex-head stainless lag screws and washers; use longer 1/4-inch exterior-rated stainless lags if the measured spacer requires them. ELEY requires at least one inch of lag penetration into wood. Predrill the framing with a 1/8-inch bit to 1-1/2 inches, or to the depth specified for a substituted longer fastener.
6. **Seal the penetrations.** Put premium exterior silicone at the structural wall around each pass-through, push the rigid spacer into it, and wipe away squeeze-out. This is the water seal ELEY specifies. Do not fill lap drainage paths or treat a cosmetic bead around the plate as flashing. If installation requires cutting out siding for a wood platform rather than four small pass-throughs, flash the block at its head and integrate that flashing with the water-resistive barrier; WRCLA says caulk alone is unacceptable at wall penetrations.
7. **Fasten the plate.** Put each lag through a 1/4-inch washer, the plate, and its spacer, then into the stud. Tighten with a 7/16-inch socket or wrench until the plate cannot move. Do not overtighten. The rigid spacers, not the cedar, carry the clamp force.
8. **Use ELEY's platform method if studs are not 16 inches apart.** For 24-inch centers, ELEY specifies a pressure-treated nominal 2x10 at least 27 inches long; for 19.2-inch centers, a pressure-treated nominal 2x12 at least 22 inches long. Attach the platform through pass-through spacers to the framing, then attach the reel plate to the platform with four 1/4 by 2-inch lags and washers. Isolate ELEY's aluminum from pressure-treated wood with its standoffs or stainless washers. A properly flashed, prefinished cedar or compatible composite mounting block is preferable aesthetically, but its structural fasteners still must reach studs or added blocking.
9. **Assemble and connect the plumbing.** Follow ELEY's manual for the arm, axle, swivel, hose strap, and brake. Hand-tighten the lead-free brass swivel to the leader hose and the garden hose to the swivel. Connect the female end of the included 6.5-foot, 5/8-inch polyurethane inlet hose to the hose bib with its washer present; start by hand to avoid cross-threading. ELEY says a leaking faucet connection should be cleaned, checked for a seated washer, and tightened only 1/4 to 1/2 turn beyond hand-tight if needed. Keep any code-required hose-bib vacuum breaker/backflow device in place. ELEY does not document the reel as a substitute for one.
10. **Load-test and inspect.** Turn on the faucet, fill the hose, inspect the bib, swivel, and all four wall penetrations, then pull the hose in its intended direction while watching for plate movement. Recheck after the first few uses and seasonally. Shut off, drain, and remove the reel from its axle for freezing weather if desired; the wall plate can remain.

## Stud-finding method

### Preferred: locate inside, transfer outside, then verify

ELEY calls exterior stud detection difficult and documents this method:

1. Put painter's tape outside near the intended plate center. Use a window glass edge as the common reference, not the trim, whose widths can differ inside and out. Use a level or plumb bob if the reference and mark are at different heights.
2. Measure from that glass edge to the exterior mark. Reproduce the measurement from the same glass edge indoors.
3. Find stud edges and centers indoors with an electronic finder, a strong neodymium magnet over drywall screws, or trim fastener locations. Scan from both directions and confirm repeated readings. Studs are 1-1/2 inches wide.
4. Measure each stud center relative to the transferred mark. Reverse left and right when returning outside because the observer now faces the opposite side of the wall.
5. Expect 16-inch centers, but measure rather than infer. ELEY notes 24-inch and, less often, 19.2-inch spacing. Corners and window openings contain extra framing, so they are useful references but not reliable proof of the next center.
6. Look for vertical lines of cedar siding nails as supporting evidence. WRCLA requires horizontal cedar siding to fasten to studs or furring, at no more than 24-inch centers over wood sheathing and 16-inch centers without it. Nail lines can also land on furring or blocking, so do not treat them as proof.
7. Verify with ELEY's extra-long 3/32-inch pilot-bit test. After passing only through the siding, mark another 1-3/4 inches on the bit and drill. Continuous wood chips/resistance through that depth confirms framing. A void or sudden loss of resistance means stop; do not enlarge the hole.

### Tool limits

An electronic finder may be used outside as a cross-check, not the deciding measurement. Franklin Sensors states that its M150/M92/M90 senses density differences to 1.5 inches but explicitly says detection through exterior materials is not guaranteed because thickness and density vary. Cedar laps create exactly that uneven density. Zircon's Pro55 DeepScan limit is likewise 1-1/2 inches, which is a maximum sensing depth rather than a promise through siding, sheathing, and a rain-screen gap.

A strong magnet does not detect wood. It finds ferrous siding nails or screws. On cedar it may reveal vertical fastener lines, but stainless cedar fasteners may be only weakly magnetic or nonmagnetic, and a detected fastener may attach to sheathing or furring rather than a stud. Use magnet and nail lines to narrow the search, then transfer from inside and prove the stud with the pilot hole. This is slower than believing the first beep. The wall remains attached afterward, which seems useful.

## Tools and hardware

### Supplied with the reel

- Wall plate and four weather-resistant 1/2-inch lap-siding standoffs
- Four 1/4 by 2-1/2-inch stainless hex-head wood lag screws and flat washers
- Four masonry sleeve anchors, not used for a wood-framed cedar wall
- 6.5-foot by 5/8-inch inlet/leader hose with lead-free brass fittings
- Assembly hex key

### Bring or buy

- Tape measure, pencil, painter's tape, level or plumb bob
- Indoor electronic stud finder and strong neodymium magnet
- Drill/driver; extra-long 3/32-inch wood bit for verification; 1/8-inch wood bit for lag pilots
- Hole saw sized to the selected pass-through conduit; drill stop or tape to control depth
- Schedule 80 rigid conduit for four custom spacers, schedule 40 if schedule 80 is unavailable; saw and deburring tool
- Four longer 1/4-inch stainless exterior structural lag screws if spacer length plus 1-3/4 inches exceeds 2-1/2 inches
- 7/16-inch socket/ratchet or wrench; Phillips screwdriver; 3/8-inch wrench for brake adjustment
- Premium exterior silicone compatible with cedar, sheathing/WRB, and spacer material; caulk gun
- Eye protection and shop vacuum
- Hose washers; pliers used only for the small final faucet tightening ELEY permits
- If required by the existing plumbing or local code, a listed hose-bib vacuum breaker installed upstream of the leader hose
- For non-16-inch framing: ELEY-sized platform, exterior finish, isolation washers/standoffs, and head flashing integrated with the WRB
