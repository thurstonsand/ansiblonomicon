# Smoke and CO candidate screen

Initial screen as of 2026-09-07. This is not the final alarm design.

## Safety gate

For a primary US residential alarm, require the exact purchased model to bear a current mark from a nationally recognized testing laboratory for both ANSI/UL 217 smoke-alarm and ANSI/UL 2034 carbon-monoxide-alarm standards. A Matter, FCC, CE, EN 14604, or EN 50291 certification does not substitute. The likely governing jurisdiction is Cobb County, Georgia; Georgia's 2024 Residential Code requires combination alarms to be listed to UL 217 and UL 2034. The design must also satisfy the applicable placement, mains-power, backup-power, and whole-house interconnection requirements without Home Assistant, a vendor cloud, Protect, Wi-Fi, Thread, or an automation carrying the alarm between devices.

“Certified to a UL standard” is not necessarily “UL Listed” by UL Solutions. An ETL Listed model tested to the same ANSI/UL standards is an independently listed device; confirm that the exact model's label and listing are acceptable to the authority having jurisdiction.

## Current direction

Inspect the installed alarm circuit before choosing. Record every alarm's exact model, manufacturing date, power source, breaker/circuit, interconnect conductor, and required replacement locations. If the house has conventional 120 V interconnect wiring, PLACE presently offers the cleanest new-product safety architecture in this group; established First Alert or Kidde alarms remain the boring baseline.

UniFi is the preferred smart candidate because Protect is already established and its integration is the best fit. The current US specification resolves the earlier European-certification concern: USL-Smoke-US claims the two listings Georgia requires. Accept the advertised daisy-chain capability for selection and prove it during commissioning rather than holding up the choice for more documentation. The remaining design decision is whether the existing wiring assessment allows battery-only replacement; if not, retain a listed hardwired safety layer and use UniFi as additional coverage and smart observation. Owl, Phare, and Sensereo stay out unless their US certification status materially changes.
