# Smart blackout blinds

## Verdict

Use **SmartWings Matter-over-Thread blackout shades for the ordinary rectangular windows**, subject to a one-window trial, and treat the skylights and odd window as separate product classes. SmartWings is the strongest value fit on paper: made-to-measure blackout rollers start around $190, its skylight cellular line has blackout fabric, and both offer Matter-over-Thread motors without a proprietary bridge. The existing Apple TV/HomePods can provide Thread border routing and Home Assistant can commission the shades through its certified Matter integration. The one-window trial matters because a standards-based integration is not the same evidence as Lutron's long field history.

For **VELUX-branded skylights**, prefer matching VELUX solar room-darkening shades and a KLF 200. Home Assistant's official VELUX integration talks locally to the KLF 200. For other rectangular skylights, compare SmartWings' Matter skylight cellular shade against a Somfy-powered tensioned shade from a local fabricator. Do not assume any ordinary roller shade can run on a slope: it needs tracks, tension, or another mechanism that positively moves and retains the fabric.

The **oddly shaped window cannot be selected until its shape is known**. A normal roller product from IKEA, Eve, SwitchBot, SmartWings' standard lines, Serena, or ordinary Somfy fabricators is rectangular. Hunter Douglas has the broadest first-party specialty-shape catalog, but motorization depends on the exact product and shape. Lutron's full-line dealer channel offers tensioned angled systems, while consumer Serena explicitly does not offer skylights. Get shape-specific drawings from Hunter Douglas and a Lutron/Somfy dealer rather than accepting “custom size” as “custom shape.” Those are very different promises, as windows have arranged for us to discover.

If absolute connectivity confidence outweighs cost, **Lutron remains the baseline**. Serena plus Smart Bridge has a mature local Home Assistant path, long battery claims, and physical controls, but Serena does not solve skylights or arbitrary shapes. Full-line Lutron can solve tensioned skylights at dealer pricing.

## Option notes and primary sources

### IKEA FYRTUR class

FYRTUR is the cheap Zigbee reference, not a custom-window system. IKEA says it blocks outside light, uses a rechargeable BRAUNIT battery, needs its included repeater within 10 m, works with DIRIGERA, comes in fixed sizes, and **cannot be cut**. That makes it unsuitable for skylights and the odd-shaped window. Availability in IKEA US appears inconsistent, so verify that a current replacement model exists before designing around it.

Home Assistant's [ZHA integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/zha/) supports Zigbee devices directly with a coordinator such as SkyConnect. This avoids DIRIGERA, but it is an HA integration path rather than one IKEA promises. Sources: [IKEA FYRTUR product page](https://www.ikea.com/ma/en/p/fyrtur-block-out-roller-blind-smart-wireless-battery-operated-grey-90408170), [Home Assistant ZHA](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/zha/).

### SmartWings

SmartWings sells made-to-measure blackout rollers with Matter-over-Thread, Zigbee, and Z-Wave motor choices. Its 100% blackout Vigor page lists $189.99, a rechargeable motor with a 4–6 month claim, USB-C charging, and optional solar. The standard roller is rectangular. SmartWings separately sells tracked skylight cellular shades, which is the relevant product for sloped glazing; the Matter motor can use the existing Apple border routers and HA's Matter controller.

The price and standards fit are unusually good. The uncertainty is operational evidence: Home Assistant's Matter implementation is official and certified, but SmartWings is not the same vertically controlled radio, bridge, and shade system as Lutron. Buy one ordinary-window shade first and exercise it for several weeks before ordering the room.

Sources: [SmartWings Vigor blackout roller](https://www.smartwingshome.com/products/smartwings-motorized-roller-shades-100-blackout-vigor), [SmartWings skylight cellular shade](https://www.smartwingshome.com/products/smartwings-motorized-premium-skylight-cellular-shades-hazel), [Home Assistant Matter](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/matter/), [Home Assistant Thread](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/thread/).

### Eve MotionBlinds

Eve offers both a $199.95 retrofit motor and a made-to-measure US collection starting around $330. The collection includes blackout fabrics. Both use Matter over Thread and a built-in USB-C rechargeable battery. The retrofit has explicit tube and minimum-width requirements; neither product is a tracked skylight or specialty-shape system.

This is the cleanest direct-Thread alternative to SmartWings for ordinary rollers, but it starts higher and does not solve the difficult windows. Sources: [Eve MotionBlinds technical details](https://www.evehome.com/en-us/eve-motionblinds), [Eve Blinds Collection](https://www.evehome.com/en-us/eve-blinds-collection), [Eve US launch and pricing](https://www.evehome.com/en/news/eve-blinds-collection-usa), [Home Assistant Matter](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/matter/).

### SwitchBot

SwitchBot's adjustable Roller Shade covers widths from 22.8 to 72.8 inches. It has replaceable fabric, a rechargeable battery rated up to eight months, optional solar, and Matter connectivity **through Hub 2**. The manufacturer warns that inside mounting leaves side gaps and recommends an outside mount with about 10 cm overlap for complete blackout. It is a gravity roller, so it cannot serve a skylight or non-rectangular opening.

The official [Home Assistant SwitchBot integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/switchbot/) is local Bluetooth for supported products; the alternative is SwitchBot's bridge-to-Matter route through Hub 2 and HA's Matter integration. Because the Roller Shade is newer than several devices listed in HA's integration history, verify that HA exposes open, close, stop, position, and state before buying multiples. Sources: [SwitchBot Roller Shade](https://us.switch-bot.com/products/switchbot-roller-shade), [Home Assistant SwitchBot](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/switchbot/), [Home Assistant Matter](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/matter/).

### Somfy

Somfy supplies motors and controls rather than one canonical shade. That breadth is useful for skylights: a fabricator can pair a Somfy motor with tracks, tension, blackout fabric, wired power, battery, or solar. It also means “Somfy” is not a complete quote. Require the exact motor radio, fabric, side retention, power source, gateway, and feedback behavior in writing.

For HA, specify a current TaHoma gateway and enable its local developer API. Home Assistant's Overkiz documentation lists Somfy TaHoma among supported vendors and local API support for current TaHoma models. It also notes that some devices do not broadcast status changes, so reliability and state feedback depend on the selected motor. Sources: [Somfy motorized shades](https://www.somfysystems.com/en-us/products/shades-blinds-curtains/motorized-blinds-shades), [Somfy power options](https://www.somfysystems.com/en-us/products/shades-blinds-curtains/powering), [Home Assistant Overkiz](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/overkiz/).

### VELUX

If the skylights have a VELUX data plate, start here. VELUX sells model-matched solar and electric shades; its current skylight systems include a solar-powered room-darkening shade. The positive retention and model-specific fit are more important than using one shade brand throughout the room.

Home Assistant's official VELUX integration controls devices through the KLF 200 locally. Confirm KLF 200 availability and compatibility with the exact shade model before ordering, since VELUX also sells app-oriented gateways with different integration paths. Sources: [VELUX shades](https://www.veluxusa.com/products/accessories/shades), [Home Assistant VELUX](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/velux/), [VELUX KLF 200](https://www.velux.com/klf200).

### Hunter Douglas PowerView

Hunter Douglas is the best first stop for the odd window because Duette cellular products cover many specialty shapes and the company has dedicated skylight products. But the catalog's ability to _cover_ a shape does not prove PowerView can _move_ that shape. Ask the dealer to mark motorized versus fixed/manual sections on the shop drawing.

HA has a built-in local PowerView integration with covers and scenes. Its documentation records generation-specific behavior, including Gen 1/2 remotes that do not report shade position. PowerView Gen 3 also supports manufacturer-mediated Matter gateway integration, but use HA's native local gateway path unless a specific Gen 3 feature requires Matter.

Sources: [Hunter Douglas skylight shades](https://www.hunterdouglas.com/stories/buyers-guides/window-treatments-skylight-shades), [Hunter Douglas specialty shapes](https://www.hunterdouglas.com/window-treatments/specialty-shapes), [Hunter Douglas motorized shade pricing FAQ](https://www.hunterdouglas.com/faqs/how-much-do-motorized-blinds-cost), [Home Assistant PowerView](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/hunterdouglas_powerview/), [Hunter Douglas Gen 3 smart-home connections](https://help.hunterdouglas.com/hc/en-us/articles/39343059678868-Connect-PowerView-Gen-3-to-Smart-Home-Devices).

### Lutron Serena and full-line Lutron

Serena is the control: proprietary Clear Connect radio, Smart Bridge, excellent local HA support, blackout choices, and battery or wired configurations. The HA integration explicitly exposes Lutron shades as covers. Serena's own FAQ says its honeycomb line has no skylight option and directs skylight buyers to a full-line dealer for hard-wired tensioned rollers. Standard Serena is also rectangular, so it does not answer the odd window.

Full-line Lutron roller systems do offer tension systems for angled and skylight applications. They retain the likely reliability advantage and the known cost problem. Keep this as the fallback if the trial Matter shade is not boringly dependable or if a dealer-engineered difficult-window solution is worth paying for.

Sources: [Serena FAQ](https://www.serenashades.com/faq/serena-shades), [Serena fabrics and starting-price indication](https://www.serenashades.com/browse-colors/), [Lutron full-line roller shades](https://www.lutron.com/us/en/window-treatments/shades/roller-shades), [Home Assistant Lutron Caséta](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/lutron_caseta/).

## Measure before pricing

Record each opening separately. Photos should include the whole wall and close-ups of the frame, corners, labels, crank/handle, and nearby outlets.

- **Window identity:** room/window ID; manufacturer and model from any sash or skylight data plate; fixed or operable sash; indoor or outdoor shade location.
- **Exact geometry:** shape name and a sketch; width at top, middle, and bottom; height at left, center, and right; every angled side; arch radius or a full-size template for irregular curves; diagonals to reveal an out-of-square opening.
- **Mounting space:** inside-recess depth; flat head/jamb/sill width; obstructions; handle and lock clearance; trim projection; distance from glass; whether an outside mount can overlap at least 2–4 inches per side.
- **Skylights:** glass angle from horizontal; visible glass and frame dimensions; shaft depth; reachable mounting points; existing tracks; glazing temperature constraints; data-plate code. A vertical roller specification is invalid here unless the vendor supplies positive retention or tension.
- **Blackout target:** “dark enough for TV,” room-darkening, or sleep-grade blackout; acceptable edge halo; cassette/fascia preference; side channels allowed; wall overlap available. Blackout fabric alone does not stop edge leakage.
- **Power:** outlet location; route for low-voltage wire or Ethernet; attic/shaft access; solar exposure throughout the year; ladder/scaffold height for battery charging; acceptable charge interval.
- **Radio:** distance and building materials to SkyConnect, HomePods, Apple TV, and proposed proprietary hub; whether a mains-powered Thread/Zigbee router can sit nearby; required behavior during internet and HA outages.
- **Operation:** individual versus grouped movement; physical wall/hand remote; exact position feedback; stop command; local schedules; manual operation during failure; noise tolerance.
- **Quote comparability:** shade model, fabric and opacity, motor model/protocol, hub, remote, power supply/solar panel, cassette, side channels, freight, installation, programming, warranty, and any lift/scaffold charge as separate line items.
- **Odd window proof:** require a dimensioned shop drawing that says which portion moves, where fabric stacks, whether light gaps remain, and whether PowerView/Lutron/Somfy automation applies to that exact configuration. “Specialty shape available” is not enough.
