# Difficult-to-close doors

## Verdict

Do not start by moving every strike plate. First classify each door while the failure is present. A door that swings freely but needs a final shove is usually fighting the latch/strike, deadbolt strike, weather seal, or threshold. A door that rubs before reaching the latch is an alignment problem. Fix hinge or frame alignment before cutting the slab or moving the strike, because moving the strike can conceal sag without correcting it.

The front door is complete only when it closes into the latch and the smart deadbolt extends and retracts smoothly without pushing, pulling, or lifting the door. Schlage makes that an explicit prerequisite for an automatically extending bolt. The lock motor is not a door-alignment mechanism. An intermittent second attempt is also a failure: Schlage documents that repeated high-power retries increase battery use.

## Survey every door

Record the room, swing direction, where it contacts, whether the behavior changes with weather, and the results of these tests:

1. With the door open, operate the latch and deadbolt by hand. Stiffness here is in the hardware, not the frame: clean or replace the latch/bolt according to its manufacturer before adjusting the door.
2. Hold the handle so the spring latch stays retracted and close the door slowly. If it still needs pressure during the last inch, inspect weatherstrip, stops, threshold, and frame twist. If it closes freely now, mark the spring latch with painter's tape, chalk, or lipstick and find where it touches the strike.
3. With the door resting in the latched position, operate the deadbolt manually. Mark the bolt to locate any strike contact. It must extend fully without using the bolt to pull the door inward.
4. Inspect the reveal around the slab. A diagonal or tapered gap, a dropped latch, or a door that closes when lifted points to hinge sag; one isolated rub mark indicates a local high spot; uneven weatherstrip contact from top to bottom suggests a twisted or out-of-square jamb.
5. Tighten loose hinge screws by hand and retest before changing anything. Change one variable at a time and preserve the original strike outline with pencil or tape.

## Type 1: swings freely, but latching needs pressure

Use the held-handle test to separate two common cases.

### Latch or bolt hits the strike

- For a small vertical or sideways miss with even door gaps, loosen and reposition an adjustable strike. If the strike is not adjustable, move it only far enough to center the latch or bolt, filling old screw holes before redrilling so the screws do not return to the old position.
- For very slight contact, enlarge the pocket only where the transfer mark proves interference. Do not indiscriminately file away the security strike. Schlage requires its bolt pocket to provide the lock's full extension—1 inch on the cited Sense installation—and offers a strike with an additional 0.060 inch / 2 mm clearance when minor rubbing remains.
- If the door must be lifted to latch, or the reveal shows sag, repair the hinges first rather than lowering the strike to follow the sag.
- If the latch itself is stiff with the door open, or its bevel faces the wrong direction, service or replace the latch before touching the frame.

### Weather seal, stop, or threshold resists the last inch

- Look for weatherstrip that is folded, painted, detached, newly oversized, or compressed much harder at one point. Reseat or replace damaged weatherstrip; use the door manufacturer's profile rather than stacking generic foam onto the frame.
- If the hardware has an adjustable strike tab, adjust it outward in small increments to reduce compression while retaining an even seal. Andersen documents bending the strike tabs in opposite directions to increase or decrease weatherstrip compression.
- On an adjustable exterior threshold, make tiny, even changes and retest the sweep. Therma-Tru's check is that a dollar bill under the closed door should pull out with slight resistance but without tearing. Do not lower the threshold enough to create daylight or water entry.
- If the door contacts the seal at the top or bottom first, the remedy is jamb/frame realignment, not thinner weatherstrip at that one point.

### Front-door priority procedure

1. Identify the door and hardware manufacturer from labels on the hinge edge, top, sill, lock face, or glass spacer. A metal-and-glass entry may use adjustable hinges, pivots, a continuous hinge, or a multipoint lock; its model-specific adjustment wins over generic wood-door methods.
2. Run the open-door, held-handle, latch-mark, and deadbolt-mark tests above. Check whether pressure is required to catch the spring latch, to throw the deadbolt, or both.
3. Correct sag or uneven frame contact first. Then center the latch and deadbolt strikes. Finally set weatherstrip compression and threshold height just tightly enough to seal.
4. From inside with the key available, verify the manual thumbturn through ten closed-door cycles. Then verify ten automatic locking cycles. Every cycle must latch the door first and extend the bolt fully on the first attempt, without touching the door. Repeat after a large humidity or temperature change before calling it seasonally reliable.
5. Use a door or glazing professional if the leaf is frameless glass, the hinges/pivots lack documented adjustments, the frame itself must be moved, glass binds or shifts, or the multipoint mechanism does not engage evenly. Do not plane, grind, bend, or drill an unidentified metal-and-glass assembly.

## Type 2: door catches on the frame

- Tighten loose hinge-leaf screws first. Replace a stripped short screw at the top jamb hinge with one manufacturer-appropriate long screw that reaches framing; drill a pilot hole and stop when the reveal becomes even. Therma-Tru specifically prescribes long top-hinge screws into the stud for a sagging exterior door. Do not keep driving until the jamb bows.
- If the hinge is sound but the reveal is wrong, shim behind the appropriate hinge leaf in small increments. Retest latch alignment after each adjustment.
- If the jamb is twisted or out of square, or the sill is not level, reshim and fasten the frame. This is usually a carpenter/door-installer job because changing one leg can affect the seal and lock along the full height.
- Plane or sand only a wooden door with a proven local or seasonal high spot after hinge and frame faults are ruled out. Remove the minimum material and immediately reseal every exposed edge. Do not plane metal, fiberglass, fire-rated, glass, or unknown composite doors.
- If rubbing appears only during humid weather, document the location and wait to remove material until the door is at its swollen state; otherwise the dry-season gap may become excessive.

## Tools for the first pass

- Phillips and flat-blade hand screwdrivers
- painter's tape, pencil, chalk, or lipstick for contact transfer
- flashlight, straightedge, and tape measure
- assorted manufacturer-compatible hinge screws and wood toothpicks/dowels plus glue for stripped interior wood holes
- thin hinge shims

Add drill bits, chisels, or files only after a marked interference point proves they are needed. Safety glasses are not negotiable. Doors remain committed to gravity.
