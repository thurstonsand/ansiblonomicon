# Basement dehumidifier sizing and maintenance

## Verdict

Start with a current DOE-rated 50-pint/day ENERGY STAR portable, gravity-drained to a floor drain, unless the worksheet below shows that the basement is over 2,000 square feet, stays above 75% RH, has several closed-off finished rooms, or needs the appliance's noise and heat kept out of occupied space. A 50-pint portable is the sensible baseline because it reaches the top of ENERGY STAR's recommendation for a wet space under 2,000 square feet and currently costs roughly $230 to $330 with Wi-Fi or a pump. Do not use a manufacturer's “up to 4,500/7,000 square feet” marketing claim as the load calculation.

Move to a professional basement or ducted unit when distribution, noise, cold-weather operation, service life support, or capacity actually requires it. The representative AprilAire E080 and Santa Fe Ultra70 classes have larger blowers, duct connections, corrosion-resistant coils, no bucket, low-voltage control terminals, longer warranties, and roughly 70 to 80 old-test pints/day of capacity. Equipment alone commonly sits around the $1,500 to $2,000 class before ductwork, electrical work, a condensate pump, and labor, versus about $230 to $330 for a large portable. That makes the equipment delta roughly $1,200 to $1,800, with installed cost necessarily site-specific. Manufacturer-direct portable pricing supports the lower range, but the whole-basement manufacturers sell through contractors and do not publish a direct installed price, so contractor quotes must settle the real delta rather than a suspiciously precise internet estimate.

Target 45% to 50% RH in the representative occupied part of the basement and verify that remote corners remain below 60%. EPA says below 60%, ideally 30% to 50%; ENERGY STAR calls 30% to 50% generally optimal. ASHRAE's HVAC handbook says habitable spaces should remain between 30% and 60% RH at normal room temperatures, while its moisture guidance also stresses dew point and cold-surface RH because room RH alone can miss condensation. A 45% to 50% setting leaves margin for sensor error and damp corners without needlessly running the machine toward 30%. A dehumidifier controls airborne moisture. It does not repair seepage, grading, gutters, plumbing leaks, wet materials, or an unvented dryer. Those remain water problems with an electrical appliance placed nearby, which is not an improvement.

## Read capacity labels correctly

Capacity means pints removed in 24 hours under a defined laboratory condition, not bucket size and not necessarily what the unit will collect in this basement. Temperature and starting RH change real removal substantially.

DOE's June 2019 rules changed portable testing from 80°F to 65°F and replaced Energy Factor with Integrated Energy Factor, which includes off-cycle energy. The cooler test produces lower, more realistic basement ratings. ENERGY STAR gives these approximate portable equivalents:

| Through-2019 rating at the old test | Current approximate rating |
| ----------------------------------: | -------------------------: |
|                        30 pints/day |               20 pints/day |
|                        50 pints/day |               30 pints/day |
|                        70 pints/day |         40 to 45 pints/day |
|                        90 pints/day |               55 pints/day |

Thus a current “50-pint” portable is larger than the ubiquitous old “70-pint” class. Compare the DOE test rating and IEF, not whichever larger “maximum” number appears in an online title. For example, hOmeLabs advertises one machine as both 50 pints/day and “120 pint MAX at 95°F, 90% RH.” The former is the useful standardized shopping number.

Whole-home listings still require care. Santa Fe specifies the Ultra70 as 70 pints/day at 80°F/60% RH but only 52.45 at 73°F/60% RH. ENERGY STAR's rough old-to-new whole-home conversion maps 80 old-test pints to about 70 current pints. Record the test condition beside every quote so two equal-looking numbers do not impersonate equal performance.

Sources: [ENERGY STAR capacity and test change](https://www.energystar.gov/products/dehumidifier_testing_and_capacity), [DOE 2023 test-procedure final rule](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2023-07/dehum-tp-fr.pdf), [Santa Fe Ultra70 specifications](https://www.santa-fe-products.com/product/santa-fe-ultra70-dehumidifier/), [hOmeLabs 50-pint product page](https://www.homelabs.com/products/4-500-sq-ft-wifi-enabled-energy-star-dehumidifier).

## Sizing worksheet

Fill this in during humid weather, with the existing dehumidifier off long enough to observe the untreated space. One full day is a useful snapshot; several days spanning dry and rainy weather are better. Keep exterior doors and windows in their normal state.

### 1. Describe the load

- Conditioned basement floor area: `_____ sq ft`
- Ceiling height: `_____ ft` (a materially taller-than-8-foot basement adds air volume)
- Basement temperature range: `_____ to _____ °F`
- Finished, semi-finished, or unfinished: `_____`
- Open plan or number of normally closed rooms: `_____`
- Laundry dried here, shower, gym, or other moisture spikes: `_____`
- Visible seepage, sweating walls/floor, damp spots, plumbing leak, or musty odor: `_____`
- Available drain: `floor drain / sump / sink / none`
- Gravity fall from unit drain outlet to destination, with no uphill section: `_____`
- Existing unit's current DOE-rated capacity, if known: `_____ pints/day`
- Water actually removed in 24 hours before it reaches setpoint, if measurable: `_____ pints`

Fix active water entry first. EPA specifically calls for prompt leak repair, foundation drainage, clean gutters, outward ground slope, outdoor dryer venting, and unobstructed condensate drains. Capacity cannot compensate reliably for liquid water entering the building.

### 2. Measure RH where the basement has to succeed

Use at least two independent hygrometers. Put one in the main occupied or central open area at breathing height, away from the dehumidifier's inlet and warm discharge, HVAC supply, exterior door, window, masonry wall, or laundry source. Put the other in the suspected worst normally occupied or storage zone. Add one in each closed room if doors ordinarily remain closed. The point is to measure room air, not the appliance's locally dried plume or a cold wall's microclimate.

Record at the same times for several days:

| Location               | Temperature | Morning RH | Evening RH | Rain/dry | Odor or condensation |
| ---------------------- | ----------: | ---------: | ---------: | -------- | -------------------- |
| Main area              |     `___°F` |     `___%` |     `___%` | `___`    | `___`                |
| Dampest corner/room    |     `___°F` |     `___%` |     `___%` | `___`    | `___`                |
| Additional closed room |     `___°F` |     `___%` |     `___%` | `___`    | `___`                |

ENERGY STAR recommends a separate hygrometer when needed and says to hang it near the dehumidifier. Multiple separated sensors go one step further here because a finished basement can have several air zones; sizing a single portable from its own inlet sensor can conceal a damp closed room. Compare the sensors together in one location for a day first. If they disagree, note the offsets rather than manufacturing false precision.

### 3. Select the minimum current portable capacity

Use the worst sustained untreated RH, then adjust for distribution and temperature:

| Untreated condition                                             |  Under 2,000 sq ft | Over 2,000 sq ft |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | -----------------: | ---------------: |
| Slightly/moderately damp, intermittent mustiness, 50% to 75% RH | 20 to 30 pints/day |    30+ pints/day |
| Very damp, consistent damp smell or damp spots, 75% to 90% RH   | 25 to 40 pints/day |    40+ pints/day |
| Wet, sweating/seepage or indoor laundry load, 90% to 100% RH    | 30 to 50 pints/day |    50+ pints/day |

This is ENERGY STAR's current sizing table. Choose the upper end when the basement is cool, near 2,000 square feet, has a tall ceiling, sees laundry or shower spikes, or must recover after doors open. Oversizing is preferable to undersizing, according to ENERGY STAR, but airflow still has to reach every zone.

- Table result: `_____ pints/day`
- Adjustments and reason: `_____`
- Candidate current DOE rating: `_____ pints/day`
- Candidate operating minimum temperature: `_____ °F`, which must be at or below the measured basement minimum
- Candidate IEF: `_____ L/kWh`; higher is more efficient within the same capacity class

If one open-space portable holds the remote sensor at 50% to 55% RH without running continuously, stop. If its local sensor reaches setpoint while another room remains over 60%, capacity may be adequate but distribution is not. Open doors or improve transfer airflow, use a second unit, or duct one professional unit between zones.

Sources: [ENERGY STAR buying and sizing guidance](https://www.energystar.gov/products/dehumidifiers), [EPA mold and moisture guide](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home), [ASHRAE Handbook, Humidifiers](https://www.ashrae.org/file%20library/technical%20resources/covid-19/i-p_s16_ch22humidifiers.pdf), [ASHRAE Handbook, Moisture and Mold](https://handbook.ashrae.org/Handbooks/A19/IP/A19_Ch64/a19_ch64_ip.aspx).

## Continuous drainage

A gravity hose eliminates routine bucket carrying only. The unit must sit high enough for the hose to descend continuously to an approved drain, without kinks, dips, an uphill run, or the hose end under water. A condensate pump overcomes elevation, but exchanges the geometry constraint for another machine that can clog, lose power, or fail.

Continuous drainage does **not** eliminate:

- filter and grille cleaning, coil inspection, or loss of capacity from dust;
- checking the hose, drain pan, trap where required, and destination for slime, debris, leaks, freezing, air lock, or accidental displacement;
- frost/defrost cycles and reduced output in a cold basement;
- cleaning after storage or seasonal shutdown;
- humidity verification with a separate sensor;
- fixing the moisture source;
- overflow protection. A gravity connection can leak or back up, and a pump can fail. Put a water sensor at the unit and drain/pump area if water damage matters.

Portable bucket-less modes vary. Some require the bucket to remain seated because its float/interlock still participates; Midea's Cube has an explicit bucketless mode, while its pump manual identifies dirty pump filters, blocked hoses, bucket fit, and retained bucket water as fault causes. Follow the exact model manual rather than removing a bucket because the hose looked persuasive.

Whole-home drains are not universally “attach hose and forget.” Santa Fe requires a trap for the Ultra70 and 1 inch of fall per 10 feet of 3/4-inch PVC. AprilAire provides a removable drain insert and instructs the owner to clean the accessible pan and insert with mild detergent. The plumbing is part of the appliance.

Manufacturers do not publish a dependable expected service life in the cited primary material. Do not turn warranty length into a lifespan claim. It does establish the risk split: the cited Frigidaire and Midea portables carry one-year warranties; current Santa Fe units manufactured from 2024 receive replacement coverage for operational failure for five years and parts in year six; AprilAire publishes a five-year limited warranty for this class. Budget the portable as replaceable and judge professional equipment on installed price, warranty, parts availability, and service access. Any “expected 5/10 years” number without a manufacturer reliability study is folklore wearing a necktie.

## Smart controls and Home Assistant

The cleanest options are native controls:

- Frigidaire's Wi-Fi model exposes remote power, RH setpoint, schedules, and fan speed through the Frigidaire app and advertises Alexa/Google support.
- Midea's 50-pint Cube supports its SmartHome app and voice assistants.
- Current hOmeLabs 25/32/50-pint lines include Wi-Fi models.
- Santa Fe Ultra70 has onboard controls and a low-voltage terminal block for a remote humidistat; AprilAire supports its own external Model 76 and compatible controls. A dry-contact relay connected according to the installer's diagram is a sound local HA integration for professional units because it requests dehumidification rather than cutting compressor power.

An independent HA humidity sensor is legitimate and useful for monitoring the worst zone, alerting above 60% RH, detecting a unit that runs but no longer lowers RH, and checking the built-in humidistat. Using it with a smart plug for control is only conditionally legitimate:

1. The exact dehumidifier must resume its previous operating mode after power restoration. Verify this in its manual and by a controlled test. “Auto restart” is the required behavior; many units otherwise remain off.
2. The plug must be grounded, listed, and rated for the appliance's motor/compressor load and nameplate current. The manufacturer's direction to use a properly grounded wall outlet still applies. Do not use an extension cord or unlisted adapter.
3. The automation must provide hysteresis and a minimum off time, for example request on above 55% and off below 50%, never rapid-cycle power around one threshold. Let the appliance's own humidistat and compressor protection do normal cycling when possible.
4. Power switching cannot select modes, report drain faults, or guarantee safe compressor timing after every software failure. Native Wi-Fi or low-voltage demand control beats it.

Best practical HA design: leave the unit powered and set near 50%, use remote sensors for dashboards and alerts, add a leak sensor beside the drain, and reserve the smart plug for energy monitoring or a tested fail-safe shutdown. If the portable's onboard sensor cannot control the dampest zone, a carefully delayed smart-plug controller can work, but that is the fallback, not the first design.
