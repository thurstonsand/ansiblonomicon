# Lighting design

## Question

Old house was all can lights and I hated how it looked. I want to do it right this time and have no idea how.

- learn the fundamentals: layers, color temperature, indirect vs direct, where cans are actually fine
- candidates seen: Hue tower/wall-wash lights pointed at walls for indirect light (promo material looks good, verify)
- **Yuji LED bulbs** (high CRI) in hand — evaluate vs "regular" bulbs; note Hue vs Yuji may be competing directions (smart color vs quality of light)
- get professional help (lighting designer?) — also on the electrician list
- **outdoor lighting**: it's pretty dark around the house at night; brighten it when needed, but with intelligent choices (same learning umbrella as interior)
- working buy list for the office / bath / closet / dining trials: [lighting trial buy list](../research/lighting-trial-buy-list.md)
- **wiring audit before the electrician visit**: walk each room and decide whether the lighting plan needs a new drop or box, so it lands in the one consolidated [electrician scope](01-electrician-scope.md). First case: the master closet's only light is the two overheads; the layer that fixes it is **closet task lighting** — linear LED strip in an aluminum channel under the shelf lip or along the closet rod, motion or door-switch triggered. Hardwired needs a junction box or outlet in the closet (electrician); rechargeable/battery bars need nothing and are the way to trial the placement first
- **kitchen overhead**: the pendant that hangs where a table was meant to go gets in the way; there will be no table, so replace it with something that clears the space (or remove it and let the layer plan cover that spot)
- **laundry room light**: ugly, and reads 2700K; replace with a plainer fixture at a higher color temperature (task light, 3500–4000K)
- interacts with [Smart switches and sensors](05-smart-switches-and-sensors.md): bulb-level smarts vs switch-level
